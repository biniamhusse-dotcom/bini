1. 7-Point Dermatology HPI Checklist

  • Site of onset & spread: Centripetal, centrifugal, flexural, extensor, photo-exposed.
  • Evolution: Fleeting (<24h = urticaria) vs. fixed plaques/papules.
  • Symptoms: Nocturnal itch (scabies), burning (herpes/contact), anesthesia (leprosy).
  • Prior topical steroids: Screen for tinea incognito or perioral dermatitis.

2. Critical 8-Week Drug Reaction Window

  • Always review all systemic & OTC drugs started within the past 8 weeks.
  • High-risk culprits: Allopurinol, Carbamazepine, Phenytoin, Lamotrigine, Cotrimoxazole, NSAIDs, Antiretrovirals.
  • Red flags (SJS/TEN / DRESS): Mucosal erosions, skin pain, Nikolsky (+), facial edema, fever, eosinophilia.

3. Primary vs. Secondary Morphology

  • Flat (<1cm / ≥1cm): Macule / Patch.
  • Elevated solid (<1cm / ≥1cm): Papule / Plaque; deep dermal = Nodule.
  • Fluid-filled: Vesicle (<1cm), Bulla (≥1cm), Pustule (purulent).
  • Secondary: Scale (keratin), Crust (dried exudate), Erosion (epidermal) vs. Ulcer (dermal).

4. Finger-Tip Unit (FTU) Dosing Guide

  • 1 FTU (~0.5 g) covers 2 adult palms (~2% BSA).
  • Face & Neck: 2.5 FTU | One Arm & Hand: 4 FTU.
  • Anterior or Posterior Trunk: 7 FTU each | One Leg & Foot: 8 FTU.
  • Use low-potency steroids on face, eyelids, axillae & groin.
